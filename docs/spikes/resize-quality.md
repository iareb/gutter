# Spike `resize-quality` — High-quality downscaling in every webview

**Question:** does `createImageBitmap` with `resizeQuality: 'high'` downscale pages properly on WebView2, WebKitGTK and Android?

**Answer: no, not everywhere.** On WebView2 and Android it works well. On WebKitGTK the output has the requested size but the quality option is ignored: `high` leaves strong moire. The only method that gives a clean result on all three webviews is the design's fallback, multi-step downscaling on a canvas.

Date: 2026-11-01 · Code: branch `spike/resize-quality` (not merged, built on `spike/page-protocol`) · Done together with the [`image-formats`](image-formats.md) spike

## Setup

| | Windows | Android | Linux |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Device | Desktop PC | Motorola Edge 60, Android 16 | Same PC, WSL Ubuntu 26.04 (WSLg) |
| Webview | WebView2, Chromium 156 | Android System WebView, Chromium 156 | WebKitGTK, AppleWebKit 605.1.15 |
| `devicePixelRatio` | 1 | 2.8125 | 1 |
| Build | `pnpm tauri dev`, debug | `pnpm tauri android dev`, debug | `pnpm tauri dev`, debug |

Test file: `gutter-formats.cbz`, generated by a script. Its resize pages are 2400 × 3600, the top half made of vertical 1 px black and white stripes and the bottom half of horizontal ones, one copy as PNG and one as JPEG (quality 95).

## What the spike does

Each resize page is fetched through `page://` as a `Blob` and downscaled to 727 × 1091 (scale 1/3.3, a non-integer ratio like a real page fitted to a screen) with six methods:

- `createImageBitmap(blob, { resizeWidth, resizeHeight, resizeQuality })` with `high`, `medium`, `low` and `pixelated`;
- **canvas, one step:** decode at full size, then one `drawImage` with `imageSmoothingQuality: 'high'`;
- **canvas, steps:** decode at full size, halve on a canvas until within 2× of the target, then a last `drawImage` with `imageSmoothingQuality: 'high'`.

A good downscale averages the stripes into flat grey. A poor one samples them, leaving moire. The score is the **standard deviation of brightness** inside the stripe areas: 0 is flat grey, 127.5 is pure black and white. Each method runs 3 times; the table shows the median time, which includes decoding.

## Results

Standard deviation (lower is better) and median time, PNG page:

| Method | Windows | Android | Linux |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `createImageBitmap` `high` | **0.0**, 28 ms | **0.0**, 70 ms | 88.9, 42 ms |
| `createImageBitmap` `medium` | **0.0**, 29 ms | **0.0**, 70 ms | 73.5, 26 ms |
| `createImageBitmap` `low` | 73.6, 20 ms | 73.6, 43 ms | 73.5, 45 ms |
| `createImageBitmap` `pixelated` | 127.5, 20 ms | 127.5, 40 ms | 127.5, 42 ms |
| Canvas, one step | **0.0**, 43 ms | **0.0**, 90 ms | 88.9, 55 ms |
| Canvas, steps | **0.0**, 41 ms | **0.0**, 88 ms | **0.1**, 47 ms |

JPEG page: same scores on every platform; times 33 / 91 / 23 ms for `high` and 45 / 108 / 39 ms for canvas steps (Windows / Android / Linux).

Every method returned exactly 727 × 1091: no webview ignores the resize size, only the quality.

## Findings

- **Chromium (WebView2 and Android) honors `resizeQuality`.** `high` and `medium` give the same flat grey; `low` and `pixelated` leave moire. Decoding and resizing in one call is also the fastest clean method: on Chromium the canvas fallback costs about 50% more on Windows and 25% more on Android.
- **WebKitGTK ignores `resizeQuality`.** `high` is even worse than `low`, and a single canvas `drawImage` with `imageSmoothingQuality: 'high'` is just as bad. Only halving in steps gives clean output, at 39–47 ms, comparable to the other methods there.
- **Large pages cost real time on Android.** A 2400 × 3600 page takes 70–90 ms to decode and resize. A page change within one frame needs the pages around the current one prepared in advance, as the design already assumes.
- **The result can be detected at runtime.** The score above works on any image, so the app can downscale a small generated stripe pattern at startup and pick the method that leaves no moire, without hard-coding platforms.

## Caveats

- **The test pattern favors halving.** Halving 1 px stripes exactly 2:1 averages each pair into perfect grey. On real pages multi-step halving is a standard and good method, but its quality must be checked by eye in M1 on the test corpus.
- **macOS and iOS were not tested.** WKWebView is WebKit, so it most likely behaves like WebKitGTK, but that is not verified.
- WebKit rounds `performance.now()` to 1 ms, and all builds were debug. Times are indicative.
- The score only separates averaging from sampling. It does not tell two good filters apart (e.g. bicubic from Lanczos).

## Decision

The design changes. Step 2 of the rendering pipeline no longer assumes that `resizeQuality: 'high'` works: at startup the app runs a quick self-test that downscales a generated stripe pattern with `createImageBitmap` and measures the moire left. If the result is clean it decodes and resizes in one call (Chromium); otherwise it decodes at full size and halves on a canvas (WebKit). The choice is made once per run and logged.

## Update (M1 step 1.3)

The self-test above was built and failed on the first real page: on the Android phone a 2400 px page fitted to a 1080 px screen showed strong moire, while the self-test reported 0.0. The spike, and the self-test after it, only measured a ratio of 1/3.3. Measured again in Chromium 152 at many ratios, with the same 1 px stripes (standard deviation, lower is better):

| Ratio | `createImageBitmap` `high` | Canvas: halve, then one last step | Canvas: halve, then 2× target and halve | Area average (reference) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1.3 | 73.7 | 73.5 | 36.1 | 50.6 |
| 1.7 | 54.2 | 52.2 | 16.2 | 20.4 |
| 1.9 | 42.3 | 40.3 | 5.4 | 6.7 |
| 2.2 | 25.7 | 0.0 | 0.0 | 11.7 |
| 2.5 | 11.0 | 0.0 | 0.0 | 25.5 |
| 2.8 | 1.2 | 0.0 | 0.0 | 25.8 |
| 3.3 | 0.0 | 0.0 | 0.0 | 19.9 |

Chromium's `high` is clean only from about 2.8 up. Ending every downscale with an exact 2:1 step (scale to twice the target, then halve) is clean from a ratio of 2 up, on both engines. Below 2 it depends on the engine. In the real app, standard deviation on WebKitGTK 2.52 vs Chromium: 63.3 vs 36.1 at 1.3, 30.6 vs 16.2 at 1.7, 10.4 vs 5.4 at 1.9. On WebKitGTK that is worse than an area average below about 1.8.

**New decision:** no self-test. Every platform decodes at full size and downscales on a canvas, halving and ending with an exact 2:1 step. See the rendering pipeline in the design doc.
